Financial Management of Rickshaw Pullers in Delhi

A Research Study by Nidhi Singh

Overview

This repository contains the full research paper titled “Financial Management of Rickshaw Pullers in Delhi.”
The study is based on primary data collected from 50 rickshaw pullers across Jama Masjid and Sadar Bazaar, Delhi. It investigates their earnings, expenditures, cost management, savings behavior, and socio-economic challenges.

Research Objectives

The study answers four core questions:

What does the earning of a rickshaw puller look like?
— Daily income ranges mostly between ₹150–200, with an average of ₹373/day.

What does an average expenditure structure look like?
— They spend 67.35% of their income on basic needs (food, rent, fuel, toiletries, and addictions).

How do they manage financial shortfalls?
— Mainly through loans from friends/relatives (50%) or savings (36%).

What does their basic savings structure look like?
— 74% save, often irregularly; most keep money with themselves or shopkeepers due to limited banking access.

Key Findings

Majority (36%) migrated from Bihar to Delhi.

The population is dominated by young male workers (19–40 years).

59% of rickshaw pullers are illiterate, limiting alternative employment.

Food consumes 45.76% of daily income — by far the largest expense.

Micro-loans, irregular savings, and informal credit form the backbone of their money management.

Methodology

Primary survey using structured questionnaires

Sample size: 50 rickshaw pullers

Locations: Jama Masjid & Sadar Bazaar

Timeframe: Feb–March 2022

Both quantitative (tables, distribution, averages) and qualitative (case studies) methods used
